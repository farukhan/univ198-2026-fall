The Five Parts, for a Grounded Task

The same five parts as Lectures 5 and 10. For documents, Context (which document is current) and Rules (only from the documents; NOT IN THE DOCUMENTS) carry the weight. Fill the parts; the prompt assembles on the right.

1. Task. Answer, summarise, compare, find.

2. Context. What the documents are, and which is current.

3. Input. The files, named.

4. Format. The answer, then a quote and location for each claim.

5. Rules.

Your opening turn

Why the two orange parts

Context: the model cannot tell a 2025 policy from a 2026 one, or a draft from the final; say which is current and which wins. Rules: without “only from the documents” the weights fill every gap; without “NOT IN THE DOCUMENTS” there is no honest way to say no, so it will not.

The Four Follow-ups, and Which Tool

Lecture 7's loop, with a document under it: each follow-up pushes the model back towards the window. Copy them in order. The last one is the version you check: two quotes opened, against the page.

1What else was in the window

Which passages did you retrieve and not use? List them.

Shows what the search returned. A relevant passage listed here and not used is a stray in the making.

2The stray

Which of your claims has no quote? Remove it or mark it.

Most answers contain one. This turn makes it visible.

3The miss, by keyword

Search the documents for the word “appendix”. Does anything change?

Replace “appendix” with the word you expected and did not see. A keyword search catches what meaning search missed.

4The version you keep

Give the same answer in one sentence per requirement, with the document and page in brackets after each.

Short, cited, checkable. Open two of the brackets.

Marked for the two checks and the probe, not for the answer. An answer with a failed check reported honestly scores higher than a perfect answer with no checks. Keep the chat and the files: the check must be possible later.

Before You Upload: Lecture 4, for Files and Folders

Retrieval makes uploading many files easy, and “many” is where other people's data hides. Click each item, then the bin it belongs in. Press Check.

Not even “just to summarise”.

The line most students cross this week: a classmate's annotated copy of a reading, uploaded because it was the handiest PDF. The annotations are theirs. Ask, or find the clean copy. And check a folder before the folder goes in.
