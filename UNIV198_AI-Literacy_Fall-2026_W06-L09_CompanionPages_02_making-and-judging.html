The Same Four Moves, With Pictures

Narrow

Only the beam and the wall; remove everything else.

Cuts the scene the way it cut the scope of an answer.

Correct

The second colour is green; it should be orange. Same picture otherwise.

Name the fault and the fix, not “try again”.

Expand

Add a row of three empty seats in the foreground, same style.

More, but bounded, and in the style already there.

Verify

For each value, say whether a label is visible or you estimated it.

For a read image. For a made image: you verify, by eye, every time.

The fourth move is the asymmetry of this week: a model can be made to quote the line a claim comes from; it cannot be made to prove a picture is right. That part never leaves you.

Four Questions, the Tells, and Provenance

1Correct?

A description: does it match the picture? A transcript: names, numbers, negations? A generated image: is what it shows true, if it claims to show anything?

2Complete?

What did it leave out: the small print, the second speaker, the footnote? Smoothing happens to pictures too.

3Appropriate?

Right reader, right rights (no real person, logo or living artist), right place.

4Checkable?

READ and ESTIMATED marked? Source image kept? Credentials in the file? An image you cannot trace is one you cannot use in marked work.

The usual tells (click one)

Every tell is getting rarer each year; none is proof either way. Tells buy you suspicion; certainty comes from provenance.

Provenance, in the order to check

1. Content credentials

C2PA: a signed record inside the file saying which tool made or edited it. Copilot and Gemini images carry them in 2026. Stripped by a screenshot. Check at contentcredentials.org/verify.

2. Reverse search

For a photo that claims to be real: search the image, find the earliest copy, read the context. Older than the event it claims to show is the commonest tell of all.

3. Invisible watermarks

SynthID and similar: a pattern in the pixels or audio a detector can read and you cannot see. Not every tool adds one; not every tool can read one.

The disclosure line

The line is the deliverable. Tool, date, prompt, edits, checks: twenty words. A paragraph describes the picture; the line is about the process. It is what every piece of generated media in this course carries from now on.

Takeaways

The loop works on pixels

Read the first picture for what it assumed; one change per turn; edit, do not re-roll.

A read value is a claim

Ask for READ or ESTIMATED; a precise number from an unlabelled chart is invented specifics in a new medium.

Tells, then provenance

Hands, text, reflections, consistency buy suspicion. Credentials, reverse search and watermarks buy certainty, in that order.

The disclosure line

Tool, date, prompt, edits, checks. Keep the original file. A face or a voice is somebody's: Lecture 4 before the upload.
