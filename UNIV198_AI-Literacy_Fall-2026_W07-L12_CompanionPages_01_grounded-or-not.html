The Citation Test: Exists, Says, Supports

Three questions for every cited claim, in order, thirty seconds each. A claim that fails any one is not grounded, however fluent, and however real the other citations around it are. Click a gate.

1Exists?

Open the location. Is there such a section or page, and is the quoted text actually there, word for word?

2Says?

Does the passage contain the fact as stated: the same number, name, date, negation?

3Supports?

Does the passage justify the claim at its full strength, or only part of it?

Click a gate to see how it fails, with an example from the study room policy.

One claim, three ways to fail

Section 3.3 of the policy says: “A cancellation later than 2 hours before the start, or a non-arrival under 3.2, counts as a no-show. Three no-shows in a semester suspend the group's booking rights for 14 days.” Three answers cite it. Judge each, then press Reveal.

“Three no-shows in a semester suspend booking for 14 days (Section 3.3).”

Grounded. Exists, says, supports.

“Three no-shows suspend booking for the rest of the semester (Section 3.3).”

Says fails. The number changed; the citation is real. (That wording is the 2025 policy's: a model that saw both versions blends them.)

“One missed booking can cost you your room privileges (Section 3.3).”

Supports fails. Nothing false; the claim is bigger than the passage. It takes three, in a semester, for 14 days.
The third kind is the one students miss. Every word of the passage is true; the claim built on it is not. You do not read the quote; you read the quote against the claim.

Five Kinds of Question, and How Retrieval Treats Each

Retrieval shows the model a few passages, not the document. Any question whose answer depends on all of it is answered from a sample, confidently. Sort the ten questions below into their kind, then press Check.

Lookup

good: the words match

Number or date

good: check the digit

Across documents

fair: the comparison is the model's own

Not there

fair: only with the rule

The whole document

bad: a sample, counted

The last kind is the one to remember. “How many times”, “list every”, “summarise everything”: ask per document, add up yourself, or paste the whole document when it fits (Lecture 7's window, Lecture 12's limits).

Group Study Rooms: Frequently Asked Questions

Campus Learning Commons (an invented facility for UNIV198)
Learning Commons service desk. Updated October 2026. Refers to the Group Study Room Policy, version 3.

Q1. Can two of us book a room?
No. A group is at least three members (Policy 2.1). Two students may use a free room without a booking until a booked group arrives.

Q2. We will be a few minutes late. Do we lose the room?
The room is held for 15 minutes after the start of the booking. After that it is released to the next group (Policy 3.2).

Q3. How far ahead can I book?
7 days for most rooms. Room 204 is shared with the teaching timetable and can be booked only 3 days ahead (Policy 2.2 and 5.1).

Q4. Can I bring coffee?
Covered drinks, yes. Food, no (Policy 4.1).

Q5. What if we lose a whiteboard marker?
Report it at the service desk. A replacement marker is charged at cost, currently 40 TL, against the ID card the marker was borrowed on.

Q6. Is there a fine for no-shows?
No fines. Three no-shows in a semester suspend the group's booking rights for 14 days (Policy 3.3).

Q7. Which rooms have a screen?
Rooms 102, 104, 202 and 203. Bring your own HDMI or USB-C cable.

Q8. I booked under the old rules, with 8 hours a week. What happens?
Bookings made before 1 September 2026 keep the old limit until they expire (Policy 2.3). New bookings follow the 6-hour limit.

What the Room Usually Finds

Activity 1: the audit

  • Claim 4, supports fails. Section 3.3 does say a late cancellation is a no-show; it takes three, in a semester, for 14 days. Half the room passes it on “says” and stops too early.
  • Claim 5, grounded. Room 203, 10 seats, screen. Some fail it anyway, having read the row above. The test cuts both ways.
  • Claim 6, exists fails. No Section 6.2. Plausible, formatted like the others, and nowhere in the document. Delete it.

The lesson: six claims, three failures, three different tests. A cited answer is not a checked answer; it is an answer that can be checked quickly, and that is the whole of its value.

Activity 2: the five kinds

  • Kinds 1 and 2 pass. Lookups and numbers are found and cited correctly by nearly everyone; a few digit errors, all from tables.
  • Kind 3 is the quiet one. “B agrees with A” when B agrees with a qualification A does not have. Both quotes exist and say; the comparison overreaches.
  • Kind 4 depends on the rule. With it, NOT IN THE DOCUMENTS; without it, an answer with the nearest citation.
  • Kind 5 fails and says nothing. A count, a “complete” list, delivered with confidence. Asked how many passages it saw, most apps admit a handful.

The lesson: retrieval is a search engine with a writer attached. Ask it what a search engine can find: a passage, a number, a sentence. Do not ask it what only a reader of the whole document can know.
